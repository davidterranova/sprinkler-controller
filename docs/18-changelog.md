| 2026-09-25 | **The build is sliced into iterations.** [§15](14-roadmap.md#from-the-bench-to-the-garden--the-iterations) now breaks v1 into **five iterations**, each ending with water on the garden, a SemVer tag and evidence in the repository: **1 · First water** (the MVP — the safety floor entire, the plumbing at full width, a thin `water_meter` that counts litres and detects row 8, three HA automations), **2 · Trust the record** (the one `SCHEMA_VERSION` bump, durable replayed records, rows 7 and 9), **3 · Survive the bad day** (D8 bounded resume, the clock hardened, alarms that reach a human), **4 · Edit in four fields** (FR-12, D18, D19) and **5 · Inputs from the world** (rain, freeze, restriction window). Five slicing rules make the cuts, and a definition of done applies to every iteration. **Two findings.** (a) **v0 already carries most of the MVP's firmware** — two programs, cycle-and-soak passes, both day predicates, rotation, the three verbs and Winter, the guard, the crash-loop brake, catch-up, the daily budget, `satisfied_by_manual` — so Iteration 1 is mostly hardware plus a thin meter, and the "this season" cut that told you to skip rotation and manual credit is obsolete. (b) **Counting entities per iteration reaches the ~130 ceiling in Iteration 3**, because §9's ledger neither counts the sensors the fault policy needs (latch tripped, kill-switch position, indoor leak, outdoor temperature) nor subtracts the 24 per-zone config mirrors D18 makes redundant. That fixes the order: **the editor (4) must land before the inputs (5)**, and §9's ledger is due a revision when it does. Also corrected four stale items in the v1 paragraph — the IP66 enclosure (D15), buried multicore (D16), *defer-not-truncate* (withdrawn by D4) and cycle-and-soak listed as out of scope (moved into v1 by D5 and already shipped in v0). |
| 2026-09-16 | **The Home Assistant interface is specified — and specifying it closed the entity budget.** New **[FR-12](02-functional-requirements.md#fr-12--the-home-assistant-interface-owner-request-2026-09-16)** (7 groups, 40 requirements) states what the interface must answer: global and per-zone status, what happens next, statistics for a single run and over a selectable range, and the add / configure / delete of a zone in **four fields — label, frequency per week, runs per day, minutes per run**. **Three findings.** (a) **The budget closes at 128 of ~130** — 91 published today, +32 per-zone read-outs, +6 globals, −3 v0 bench instruments, +2 for the D11 rain gauge — so *every* derivable figure had to move into HA (averages are `total ÷ count` from device-owned counters, which is also **exact across an outage** where an HA-accumulated mean is not), and **the zone editor could not be built from entities at all**. Hence **D18**: an HA-helper form pushed by one structured `api:` action, with staging, validation and Apply/Pending still **device-side** — *not* the option (c) rejected in [§9](08-home-assistant.md#why-this-is-not-the-option-c-that-was-rejected-above), and the table there says why. That makes **backlog item 1 blocking for v1**. (b) **"Frequency per week" is a property of the zone, not of the program** — new **FR-5.11** moves the day predicate off `Program`, which is a **`SCHEMA_VERSION` bump** and the reason FR-5.8's refuse-don't-guess rule exists. (c) **Three numbers in this interface are not what they look like**, and each has a requirement now: an *average per run* needs its denominator stated (a skipped run is not a zero-litre run, FR-12.1.4); a *next run* is a schedule for the first zone and a **projection** for every other one (FR-12.3.3); and **"runs per day" is not "passes"** — confusing them triples the water instead of improving infiltration (FR-12.6.4). **D19**: deleting a zone **retires** the slot and keeps its history, because there are permanently eight slots and releasing one is no reason to destroy a season of data. Dashboard respecified as **three views** — *Statut / Statistiques / Zones*. Firmware owes three things: per-zone next-run projection across the 7-day horizon, a run counter gated on "delivered water", and per-zone predicates plus labels in the schedule blob. |
| 2026-09-05 (14) | **Behaviour flowcharts added.** Six Mermaid diagrams put the parts of the design that are decision trees into a form a table cannot carry: the **fail-closed escalation ladder** ([§5](03-fault-policy.md#fail-closed-layer-by-layer)) showing which layer catches the failure of the one above it and why row 8 is the only residual; the **mode and phase state machines** ([§8](07-firmware.md#modes-phases-and-the-three-verbs)), which make Hold/Pause/Stop/Winter legible as two orthogonal dimensions rather than four buttons; the **minute tick** with the full refusal ladder in code order; the **boot and crash-recovery order**; and the **alarm lifecycle** from detection through the single diagnostic run to an attributed acknowledgement ([§11](10-alarms.md#from-detection-to-acknowledgement)). | 2026-09-06 (2) | **The PCF8574 is out of the design.** The I²C expander is removed from the pin map, the BOM, the component summary, the wiring doc and the config comments — it was carried as the recommended way to drive eight zones, and the wiring that actually got built does not want it. **The decisive argument is the guard:** `irrigation_guard` can only hard-close pins it writes directly, so behind I²C its raw sweep would cover the master valve and nothing else — and an I²C lock-up (a slave holding SDA low, which §7 already calls a real event in a noisy 24 VAC box) is precisely when that sweep matters. **The boot-safe property the expander was credited with is already present without it:** on an active-low opto board the ESP32's verified *"pins are output-disabled during reset"* leaves every `IN` pulled up and every relay off, and **T3** is what confirms it — the garage-door incident behind the original finding is an *active-high* failure, the configuration T3 exists to reject. That finding in [§7](06-buy-vs-build.md) is **left in place and marked superseded** rather than deleted, because its evidence is sound. **The pin budget closes:** 12 of the 15 usable output-capable GPIOs and all four input-only pins are allocated, leaving **4, 14, 16** — exactly the three the spare row names. **What survives is the GPIO12 hazard, and it is now purely a wiring hazard**: the guard's config schema rejects GPIO12 outright so the firmware cannot make the mistake, but GPIO12 sits next to GPIO13 (the deadman) on the 30-pin header, so label it. Costs fall accordingly — recommended build **€1 751 → €1 741**, bench order **€160 → €150**. The FR-11 per-zone button panel loses its assumed input path and is now marked **open**. |
| 2026-09-06 | **Wired for eight zones.** The relay board is 8-channel and all eight inputs are on GPIOs (**32, 33, 25, 26, 27, 23, 19, 18** — the all-direct fallback set), so the firmware now declares eight rather than four. Four are planted to start with, which costs nothing: a zone with 0 minutes in every program is left out of the resolved plan entirely, so the fifth bed is a duration edit rather than a firmware change. The guard, by contrast, knows all nine pins from the first boot — a pin that can reach a relay coil is a pin the safety layer must be able to close. **Two findings.** (a) **`default_schedule` silently defeated FR-4.5** — it gave every zone a unique priority, and rotation happens *within* an equal-priority group, so the same zone was always last for anyone who never touched priorities. Now all zones default to equal priority; the new eight-zone rotation test is what caught it. (b) **The sequencer's per-valve switches were an FR-10.1 violation** — turning one on in Home Assistant calls `start_single_valve()` directly, bypassing hold, winter, lockout, zone-lockout and the daily budget, and the main switch bypasses them the same way. All are now `internal:`; HA reaches the valves only through the supervised *Start manual run* and *Test all zones*. That also paid for the extra zones: **91 HA-visible entities at eight zones against 85 at four.** Per-zone entities moved into a `zone-ui.yaml` package included once per zone with `vars`, because eight hand-copied blocks is eight chances to write the wrong zone index under the right heading. Flash 51.5 %, RAM 31.8 %. Pin map recorded as-built in [§7](05-hardware.md#pin-map) — note the **Spare** row there is now stale. |
| 2026-09-05 (13) | **v0 built — four zones, LEDs, no water.** The roadmap's bench prototype now exists: a pure host-tested core (`components/irrigation_core/`), an independent guard (`components/irrigation_guard/`), a scheduler (`components/irrigation_scheduler/`), five YAML packages and a **61-test** host suite that runs in ~0.2 s with no toolchain. Firmware **941 KB = 51.3 %** of the 1.75 MB app partition, static RAM 30.5 %, ~85 entities. **Three findings worth recording.** (a) **The simulated season found a real bug** — latching a CRITICAL refused the *next* run but left the current one open, i.e. the valve that raised the alarm stayed open; `latch_critical()` and `set_winter()` now abort the run and report whether they did. (b) **`lib/irrigation_core/` cannot work as specified** — ESPHome only gathers sources belonging to a component package, so the core is a config-less *component* instead, keeping one copy shared by the host tests and the device build. (c) **Direct GPIO for the zones on the bench, not the PCF8574** — the guard can only hard-close pins it writes itself, and behind an expander its raw sweep would cover the master valve alone. Also: the safe-mode pin sweep is now **generated from the guard's own config** so it cannot drift, `catch_up_window` (60 min) is a new v0 decision that the requirements never bounded, and rows 7–9 and 11 of the fault policy are explicitly **deferred to v1** for want of a flow meter. Bench procedure in [§21](21-bench-procedure.md). |

| 2026-09-05 (11) | **Capacity answer refined — the constraint is not where the first pass said.** Corrected the RAM figure: ~130 entities is **≈13–32 KB, not ≈11 KB** (the earlier number counted only `EntityBase`, not the instantiated component). More importantly, **entity count is not a heap problem at all — it is a ListEntities enumeration problem**, and ~130 entities sits close to where real devices have failed: verified reports of entities silently missing after connect (~250 → only 120–140 visible) and a task-watchdog reboot from the loop spinning on a full TCP buffer. New risk **R24**; **NFR-M8 now pins a minimum ESPHome of 2026.9** (fixes #18577 and #18802/3) — note the pool controller runs 2026.7.2, predating both. Also: **alarm on `debug.block`, not `free`** — every documented failure is a contiguity failure, not a total-free-bytes one — and `min_free` requires ≥2026.2.0. |
| 2026-09-05 (10) | **Capacity question answered, and a P0 bug found in the process.** The ESP32-WROOM-32 is **comfortably sufficient** — **1.75 MB app partition [V]** against a ~1.02–1.29 MB build (**28–43 % headroom**), ~130 entities cost **≈11 KB** of a ~150–220 KB heap (`sizeof(EntityBase)` ≈ 20 B, names in flash), no entity-count ceiling; watch-thresholds documented. **But the deadman as specified could not have worked (new risk R1b):** LEDC free-runs, so a hung loop would leave the wave running and the valve rail enabled, and toggling from `loop()` is capped at ~31 Hz which the charge pump resolves to ~1.75 V, below V<sub>GS(th)</sub>. Redesigned as an **independent `esp_timer` heartbeat driving a retriggerable monostable**; **E6 upgraded** to require proving it under a deliberately hung firmware. Also: **OTA calls `App.feed_wdt()` inside its blocking loop [V]** — the watchdog is silenced while the supervisor is stopped, so OTA must be forbidden during a run. Three corrections: **NVS is 112/96 sectors, not 109/5** (the "20× weaker Arduino NVS" argument is dead), **ESP32-S3 has 4 PCNT units, not none** (only the C3 has none), and `pulse_meter` is ISR-based rather than PCNT. |

| 2026-09-05 (4) | **Found and fixed a latent safety bug surfaced by asking whether a cycle can exceed 2 h 40 — it can.** The max-runtime backstop had been specified "in the 24 VAC common"; since the master valve stays energised across a whole cycle, a 45-min latching timer there would **trip mid-cycle and shut the garden down**. Moved to the **zone-side common**, where it bounds a single zone run and the inter-zone settle gap resets it. The settle delay is consequently promoted to a safety requirement (FR-4.3). Added FR-4.7: no component may assume a bounded cycle duration; the constraint on a cycle is its *latest finish*, not its length.
