# 14. Decisions — taken and open

## Decisions taken

| # | Decision | Answer (2026-09-05) | Consequences |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** ✅ | Per-zone flow rates | **No zone below ~1 L/min.** Fed directly from the city mains via **DN25**, driving **multiple sprinklers** per zone. | Removes the biggest metering risk. Per-run volumes are comfortably measurable, drip low-flow sensing is a non-issue, and `flow_unmetered` (FR-6.6) becomes a defensive edge case rather than a design driver. **Leak detection is unaffected** and still requires a volumetric meter — a slow weep is far below any zone's running flow. Also confirms `max_concurrent_zones = 1`: a DN25 mains feed running multiple sprinklers has no spare hydraulic budget for a second zone. See [§10](09-water-metering.md). |
| **D2** ✅ | Buy vs build | **Buy the hardware, build the software.** KinCony B8 + ESPHome + a controller-agnostic external safety layer. **No custom PCB, no OpenSprinkler, no cloud controller.** Scored on your six axes plus fail-closed and HA depth; all six commercial controllers eliminated on verified structure. | **Reverses the earlier "buy OpenSprinkler and augment" advice** — verification found confirmed triacs, zero watchdog code, an expander latch a CPU reset doesn't clear, and a €368 real landed cost. Drops the 74HC238 interlock (FR-3.5 revised) and the custom PCB from scope. Adds two mandatory YAML settings. Full analysis in [§7](06-buy-vs-build.md). **This is a time decision, not a money decision** — the build costs ~165 more hours but ~€150–460 *less* cash over 10 years. |
| **D3** ✅ | Continue watering when Wi-Fi drops? | **Yes. The ESP32 must not rely on Wi-Fi to operate. HA warns; it does not intervene. A network failure must not cause a reboot.** The paramount constraint is that a valve must never remain open. | The full autonomy programme is **in scope**: RTC, on-device scheduler, persisted config, device-side totalisation. Promoted to **P0** and **P3b** in [§1](01-overview.md#1-design-principles). Makes `reboot_timeout: 0s` on both `api:` and `wifi:` a stated requirement. Rows 1–3 of [§5](03-fault-policy.md) are confirmed as specified. |
| **D4** ✅ | Watering window | **Fully delegated to the operator. No window, no latest-finish, no cap.** They set start times and durations; the cycle runs to completion however long it takes. | **Deletes** the overrun policy (FR-4.6), truncation, `min_useful_duration`, spill grace, and the `latest_finish` half of the schedule model — the most underspecified corner of requirement 5, removed rather than solved. **Adds** FR-4.6a (cycle re-entrancy: skip, don't queue). **Does not touch** the per-run hard cap or the hardware backstop — those are P0 safety limits on a single zone, and P0 does not delegate. The drought-restriction window (NFR-LEG4) survives as a *legal* constraint, shipped disabled by default. |
| **D5** ✅ | Soil type and slope | **Heavy clay.** Multiple shorter programs per day expected. | **Cycle-and-soak moves from v2 into v1**, and multiple programs per day become a v1 requirement (FR-5.7 upgraded). Clay infiltrates at roughly 3–6 mm/h *[E]* against a rotor precipitation rate of 10–12 mm/h, so a long single pass runs off — the water leaves before it soaks. **D4 made this cheap:** with no window to fit, extra passes cost nothing but wall-clock. And with 8 sequential zones the soak interval is *free* — while zone 1 rests, zones 2–8 are running. So cycle-and-soak here is simply **N passes over the zone list at duration/N each**, which is exactly ESPHome `sprinkler`'s `repeat_number`. Little new firmware. |
| **D6** ✅ | A pump, ever? | **Not now.** Possibly a future winter rainwater tank with a pump. | Design the provisions in now, since they are cheap and retrofitting is not: **a spare relay channel** (the 9th-channel module's second channel already covers it), **a spare GPIO** (11 free), **spare conductors**, and a spare manifold position. Firmware is already covered — ESPHome `sprinkler` has `pump_switch_id` with four independent lead/lag delays. Dry-run protection becomes mandatory the day a pump appears. |
| **D7** ✅ | Rainwater tank pressurisation | **Gravity feed, optionally with a pump later.** | ⚠️ **Gravity feed is incompatible with the chosen valve technology.** Diaphragm solenoids need roughly **1–1.5 bar** to seat — below ~0.5 bar they may not close at all, which is a direct **P0 violation**. Two ways out: **(a) pressurise the tank with a pump and a small vessel**, keeping one valve technology everywhere — *recommended*; or (b) spring-return motorised ball valves on the tank branch only, accepting mixed technologies and losing the "one spare fits everything" benefit. Also absolute (NFR-LEG1): **no interconnection with the potable network**, and any mains top-up needs a **total air gap (AA/AB)** — a disconnecteur BA is *not* sufficient for fluid category 5. |
| **D8** ✅ | Power cut mid-run | **Alert, resume if possible, report progress %, and ask what to do.** | **Revises FR-3.7, which said abandon-never-resume.** Resume is now in scope, but *bounded* — see FR-3.7a. Requires persisting run progress **during** the run (RTC_SLOW_MEM does not survive power loss, so this goes to NVS/FRAM at ~30–60 s intervals: ~480 writes/day worst case, ~4 sector erases/day, ~70 years of flash — acceptable, and trivial on FRAM). "Ask what to do" becomes an **actionable HA notification** (resume / skip / restart), which must have a **safe default on no answer**: abandon. |
| **D9** ✅ | Max-hold behaviour | **Auto-resume and alert.** | Confirms the recommendation: 7-day cap, actionable warning 24 h before expiry, then auto-resume. Safe for the plants, and the alert removes the surprise. FR-8.4 stands as written. |
| **D10** ✅ | Does a manual run satisfy the schedule? | **Yes, per zone.** | FR-10.7 stands: credited at **≥80 % of planned delivery**, `satisfied_by_manual`, scoped **per zone** rather than per cycle. Prevents the most common irrigation mistake — double-watering on a hot day. |
| **D11** ✅ | Rain gauge | **Yes — add an optional tipping-bucket gauge**, to avoid watering after rain. | **Removes the one structural blocker in the HA analysis.** Rain-skip becomes genuinely autonomous rather than an HA-pushed hint with a staleness timeout, which matters because your instance has no rain sensor at all and met.no exposes precipitation only inside `weather.get_forecasts`. A reed-switch tipping bucket needs one GPIO (11 spare) and the same debounce treatment as the flow meter — though at one tip per ~0.2 mm it is a far slower signal. The HA-pushed hint stays as a **fallback**, not the primary. |
| **D13** ✅ | 30 mA RCBO available? | **The owner is the electrician** and can perform any electrical work. | Removes the only step that needed a contractor. NF C 15-100 requirements (dedicated outdoor circuit, 30 mA type-A RCD, earthing, SELV valve wiring kept out of LV conduit) remain as a **specification checklist** rather than a warning, and the BOM carries no labour allowance. |
| **D15** ✅ | Location and services | **The indoor technical room housing the pool pump.** Water, network and mains present; dry and safe. | **The single largest simplification so far.** The IP66 outdoor enclosure requirement collapses — **NFR-E1/E2 (condensation, membrane breather, UV, direct sun, insect ingress) are largely void** for the controller, and condensation was ranked the #4 risk. A DIN wall cabinet replaces a sealed outdoor box. Network access makes wired Ethernet *possible* (needs a W5500/LAN8720 — the WROOM has no PHY), though of limited value given the system must be network-independent anyway. Maps to the existing, nearly-empty HA area **`local_technique`**. **And it raises the highest-value open question in the project: should the valve manifold live in that room too?** That would eliminate buried valve wiring and its splices entirely — risk R10, *"the leading cause of death for irrigation wiring"* — at the cost of longer pipe runs and more pressure drop. |
| **D14** ✅ | Budget / bill of materials | **Delivered as [§19](19-bill-of-materials.md)** in the requested form — per component: mandatory/optional, role, minimal/recommended/robust, and a pick. **Minimal ≈ €688 · ⭐ Recommended ≈ €1 751 · Robust ≈ €3 433** (TTC, labour €0 per D13, bench tooling excluded). 166 verified prices. | Recommended splits **40 % hydraulics, 21 % power+enclosure, 15 % sensors, 9 % safety layer, 6 % spares, 0 % trenching**. ~€690 of "Robust" is two parts the analysis recommends *against*. **Minimal has two lines that are dangerous rather than merely thin** — a meter that cannot detect a leak, and a latch that clears on a power cut. The BOM also found **16 places this spec was wrong**; all are now corrected in place. |
| **D16** 🔶 | Manifold location — technical room or buried valve boxes? | **Recommendation: move it indoors.** Verified pricing makes it both **cheaper (≈€360 at D = 20 m)** and far more reliable. **Conditional on the room having a floor drain (E7)** and on measuring **D (E8)** — break-even is **D ≈ 58 m**. | Jumbo valve boxes are **€100 each [V]** (not ~€30) and 13-core buried multicore ~€330 — **€625 of spend whose only purpose is surviving burial**. Against that, 7 extra runs of PE32 at €1.40/m is **€196 at D = 20 m**. **The hydraulic objection collapses:** 20 m of extra PE32 costs **~0.045 bar — less than the master valve you are already fitting** (PGV-101 = 0.1 bar) **[V]**, against a professional criterion of ≤3 m/100 m **[V]**. And it **deletes risk R10** rather than mitigating it. Master valve, filter, meter and bypass go indoors **unconditionally**, which lets the zone-valve call wait until the trench is open — **so do not pre-buy cable.** |

## Still open

Roughly in order of how much of the design collapses if the assumption is wrong.

| # | Decision | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **D17** ⏸ | **How long after a missed start may a program still begin?** v0 ships a **60-minute catch-up window**; the requirements name `catch-up` as a run source (FR-7.2) but never bound it. | The two ends are both wrong. Zero means a router reboot at 05:00 costs the day's watering. Unbounded means a fortnight's outage becomes a night of continuous watering — the failure the whole design exists to prevent. 60 min is the smallest value that survives a short power cut or an AP restart. **Not blocking**: it is one line in `packages/scheduler.yaml` and the resolver takes it as a parameter, so changing it needs no code. Worth an explicit answer before v1, because it is the only scheduling behaviour in the firmware that no requirement states. |
| **D12** ⏸ | **Does your commune's *règlement de service* require a disconnecteur BA? Does it accept a sous-compteur for the assainissement deduction?** — *deliberately left open.* | One phone call, whenever convenient. A €150–400 component decision and a possible recurring bill saving. **Not blocking**: plumb a removable spool piece at the piquage so a BA can be fitted later without cutting pipe. Becomes non-optional if fertigation is ever added (NFR-LEG10). |
